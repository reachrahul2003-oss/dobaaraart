The story behind the name

Art that returned — twice.

About the Artist

Jyothi

Jyothi picked up a paintbrush as a child the way most children pick up a pencil — naturally, without thinking. Life had other plans. School gave way to responsibilities, and the brushes were quietly set aside.

In 2016, a YouTube video changed that. She came back to painting on her own terms — teaching herself, experimenting freely, finding her way back to something she had never truly left. Then in 2020, a new kind of joy arrived: her son. The brushes rested again.

But this time, the return was different. She discovered Indian classical and folk art — Warli, Mandala, Tanjore — and enrolled in formal classes for the first time. What began as a homecoming became a transformation. Today, Jyothi paints with the confidence of someone who has earned every stroke twice over.

"Art is not what you see, but what you make others see."

"Every stroke is a return"

Dobara means again. It is not just a name — it is the philosophy. Art that paused, returned, transformed. These paintings carry the weight of that journey.
